# Strategy: Laufzettel by oesterreich.gv.at 2030

Deliverable "high-level strategic concept" (vision, target personas, value proposition) of the challenge
"Digital Assistants: Evolution to 2030 (Public Sector)". Pitch deck: see LAUFZETTEL.md.

## Vision

**"The offices walk. You don't."** In 2030 the citizen no longer carries papers from office to office.
The state notices a life event (a move, a birth, an expiring passport), prepares the errand from what its
registers already know, and hands the citizen a routing slip that fills itself. The citizen confirms
instead of filling in, and signs with their own seal. Official, understandable, and a little bit fun.

## Value proposition

- **For citizens:** fewer forms, no counter visit for clean cases, deadlines and consequences explained
  before they bite, in their language, by voice or touch. Estimated time kept per errand is shown (e.g.
  about 45 minutes for a change of residence).
- **For the administration:** clean, complete applications; the registers and third parties (landlord)
  deliver the data instead of the citizen retyping it; staff time goes to the unusual cases, which arrive
  with the walkthrough position attached.
- **For trust:** every value shows its source, only the citizen signs or pays, no ranking, no tracking.

## Conceptual framework

1. **Trigger:** a life event reaches the state (third-party confirmation, register data, a letter).
2. **Slip:** the walkthrough engine turns the service and the citizen's data into rows; offices stamp what
   they know, the citizen answers the rest.
3. **Explain:** plain language for every official term and every letter (original next to plain words).
4. **Sign:** a deliberate act by the citizen (hold the seal); the AI has no submit tool.
5. **Reward (fair nudging):** the seal grows with every stamp, the time kept is shown, deadlines are met.
   Voluntary, private, only positive, only official errands, explained openly.

## Why it is different from a chatbot

The citizen does not have a conversation; the citizen gets an errand done. The assistant's words are
captions to a visible process. Free speech is welcome, but it fills rows instead of piling up messages.
